# E-bike Market Research — Source List

This is the default source map for the `ebike-market-research` skill.

The list is a starting point, not a closed universe. Add country-specific sources when the research scope changes.

## Tier A — European official / association sources

### European Cycling Industries (ECI)
- Website: https://cyclingindustries.com/
- News: https://cyclingindustries.com/news/
- Use for: EU-level industry policy, market initiatives, industry data projects, compliance topics.

### CONEBI
- Website: https://www.conebi.eu/
- Market reports: https://www.conebi.eu/industry-market-reports/
- Use for: Bicycle Industry & Market Profile, European market data, production, sales, policy and standardization.

### ZIV — German Bicycle Industry
- Website: https://www.ziv-zweirad.de/en/
- Use for: German bicycle/e-bike market data, policy, industry outlook, technology and compliance topics.

### LEVA-EU
- Website: https://leva-eu.com/
- Use for: light electric vehicle regulation, EN 15194-related issues, Battery Regulation, trade measures, EU compliance and policy.

### European Commission
- Website: https://commission.europa.eu/
- Use for: EU legislation, battery policy, product rules, trade policy and official announcements.

### EUR-Lex
- Website: https://eur-lex.europa.eu/
- Use for: authoritative EU legal texts and amendments.

### Eurostat
- Website: https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/
- Use for: EU trade, import/export and economic statistics.

### EU Safety Gate
- Website: https://ec.europa.eu/safety-gate/
- Use for: dangerous-product alerts, recalls and safety signals.

## Tier B — Specialist industry media

### Bike Europe
- Website: https://www.bike-eu.com/
- Market data: https://www.bike-eu.com/market-data
- Use for: European market data, business, production, laws and regulations, trade, supply chain and industry analysis.

### Cycling Industry News
- Website: https://cyclingindustry.news/

## Source handling rules

1. Prefer official or original sources for regulations, statistics and company announcements.
2. Use industry media to discover and contextualize events.
3. Treat social media, forums and Reddit as weak-signal sources unless independently verified.
4. Store both publication date and event date.
5. Record the original URL.
6. Mark paywalled sources.
7. Do not copy long passages; summarize.
8. If multiple articles cover the same event, deduplicate them.
